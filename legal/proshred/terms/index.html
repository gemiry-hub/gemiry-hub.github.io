4. What shredding can and cannot reach

The App overwrites the data it can reach through Windows. Some copies of your data are beyond the reach of any app running on Windows, and the App tells you about them in its notes and details rather than claiming otherwise. In particular:

  • On SSDs, USB flash drives, and memory cards, the drive decides where data is physically written and may keep older copies in blocks that no app can address. Extra overwrite passes do not change this.
  • Other copies — backups, synced or cloud copies, email attachments, shadow copies and restore points kept by Windows, the page file, and the hibernation file — are not destroyed by shredding the file on your drive.
  • Very small files can be stored inside the file system's own records, and file names can remain in those records after deletion. A free-space wipe cannot reach either.

The App is designed to defeat recovery by ordinary file-recovery software. We do not claim, and you should not rely on, the App making data unrecoverable by every means, including forensic laboratory analysis of the physical drive. If you need that assurance, destroy or professionally sanitize the drive itself.

5. Free-space wipes

A free-space wipe temporarily fills a drive with its own files. While it runs, other programs have less room than usual, and on a slow drive a wipe can take a long time. The App keeps a reserve of space free by default and removes its files when the wipe ends or is cancelled. If the drive is disconnected, fails, or Windows stops during a wipe, some of those files may be left behind; the App tells you when it cannot confirm they are gone, and it removes leftovers from an interrupted wipe the next time you run one. Writing to flash storage uses up part of its limited write life, and the App tells you this before it starts.
