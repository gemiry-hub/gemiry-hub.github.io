Privacy Policy for Any Video Converter

Last updated: November 3, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how Gemiry Company Limited ("Gemiry," "we," "us," or "our") handles information in connection with Any Video Converter on macOS and Video Converter on Android (each, the "App"), apps for converting video files. It applies to every version of the App, including those distributed through the Mac App Store (macOS) and Google Play (Android).

In short: your video files never leave your device, and we do not collect personal information about you. The App does send anonymous usage statistics to Google Analytics for Firebase so we can see which formats people convert to and whether conversions succeed. That is on by default and you can turn it off — section 4 explains what is sent and how to stop it.

1. We do not collect personal information

We do not collect, store, or share your personal information. We do not ask you to create an account, and the App does not gather your name, email address, contacts, or other personal data to send to us. We have no database of users and no way to identify you.

The one thing the App does send is the anonymous, aggregate usage data described in section 4. It is not tied to your identity, and it never contains anything about the files you convert.

2. Your video files stay on your device

Converting, trimming, and saving video happens entirely on your device. The App reads the files you select only to perform the conversion you asked for, and writes the result where you choose. Your videos and their contents are never uploaded to our servers or shared with us or any third party.

This is also true of everything about your files. We never send file names, folder names, file paths, video titles, embedded metadata, subtitle text, chapter names, thumbnails, or file durations. Nothing that identifies a specific file leaves your device. The thumbnails the App shows in its list are made on your device and stay there.

Any settings or preferences you configure are kept on your device.

On Android, the App keeps its conversion queue on your device — including the names of the files in it and the system's access references to them — so that a batch survives if Android closes the App. The queue and your settings are never transmitted, and the App excludes itself from Android's app backup.

3. Permissions

To do its job, the App asks for access to the videos you want to convert and to the folder where results are saved. On macOS this is granted through the system file picker under App Sandbox. On Android you choose the videos, or a folder of them, in the system document picker; the App reads only what you pick. These permissions are used solely for conversion and are subject to your platform's own file access controls. You can revoke them at any time in your system settings, though the App will not be able to convert files without them.

On Android the App may also ask for:

  • Storage, on Android 8 and 9 only, and only when you save results into your phone's Movies, Pictures, or Music folders. Saving into a folder you choose needs no permission.
  • Notifications, to show a conversion's progress while it runs in the background and to tell you when it is done.

If you turn on moving originals to the bin, Android asks you to confirm before any file is moved, and nothing moves unless you agree.

The App's network access is used for one purpose only — sending the usage statistics in section 4 — and for nothing else. There is no cloud conversion, no remote processing, and no way to load video from a URL or a stream. On Android, the Internet permission exists for Google Analytics for Firebase alone; purchases and the rating card go through the Google Play Store app on your device, not through the App's own network connection.

On Android the App does not ask for any location permission, for access to all files on your device, or for an advertising ID.

4. Analytics, and how to turn it off

The App uses Google Analytics for Firebase to collect limited, anonymous usage statistics. We use these only to understand how the App is used in aggregate — which output formats and codecs matter, and whether conversions are succeeding — never to identify you.

In the terms used by the app stores' data-safety labels, the SDK may collect the following, all for the purpose of analytics:

Data category Data type Purpose
App activity App interactions (which features are used, app opens) Analytics
Device or other IDs A random, per-install app instance ID Analytics
Location Approximate location (country, region, and city) derived from IP address Analytics
Financial info Purchase history (that an in-app purchase happened; no payment details) Analytics

What is sent on macOS. Two events of our own:

  • when you start a conversion — the output format, codec, and quality setting, and a rough size band for the batch (for example "2–5 files" rather than the exact number);
  • when a batch finishes — the output format, and how many files succeeded and failed.

What is sent on Android. Analytics is included only in the version of the App distributed through Google Play. It sends these events of our own. Each carries short labels and rounded counts, never anything you typed or anything taken from your files:

Event When What it carries
Files added You add videos Where they came from (picked, a folder, or shared) and rounded counts of files added and duplicates
File unreadable A file cannot be read A reason label, such as "not decodable"
Conversion started A conversion begins Output format, codec, quality, subtitle choice, what started it, a rounded file count, whether a setting was adjusted, and how many run at once
Conversion completed A conversion ends Output format, rounded counts converted and failed, and an overall result
Conversion cancelled You stop a conversion Rounded counts converted and released
Conversion failed A file fails A reason label and the output format
Pro screen viewed You open the Pro screen The place you opened it from
Purchase started You tap to buy The plan (monthly, yearly, or lifetime) and the place you opened the Pro screen from
Purchase restored You tap Restore purchases Whether Google Play found a purchase, none, or a pending one
Rating prompted The App asks Google Play to show its rating card Whether Google Play started it, and a rounded count of earlier successful conversions
Rate tapped You tap Rate in Settings Nothing

The Android App also reports which screen is open (the queue, Settings, notices, or the Pro screen) and whether you have the free version, a subscription, or a lifetime purchase. Counts are rounded into ranges: none, 1, 2–5, 6–20, 21–100, and more than 100.

Alongside those, Firebase collects a standard set of its own signals: app opens and sessions, app version, device model, operating system version, language, and approximate location (country, region, and city) that Google derives from your IP address when the data arrives. It also assigns a random app instance ID to your installation. That ID is per-install, not per-person: removing or clearing the App's data, or reinstalling it, generates a new one.

How it is set up. On Android the App turns off Firebase's collection of the advertising ID and of the Android ID, and sets Firebase's ad-storage, ad-user-data, and ad-personalisation signals to "denied". In our Google Analytics settings, Google signals and ad personalisation are off, and Google keeps event-level data for 2 months. Data is sent over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection.

If you buy the App's Pro version, Firebase also records that a purchase or subscription took place, along with the product and its price. The analytics SDK measures this automatically alongside the billing of the store you bought from — Apple's on macOS, Google Play's on Android. It tells us that a purchase happened, not who made it, and it carries no payment details — we never see or receive your card, bank, Apple Account, or Google account information.

What is never sent — your videos, any part of their contents, file names, folder paths, embedded metadata, subtitle text, or thumbnails. The App also does not use an advertising ID; on Android the permission for it is removed from the build, so Google never receives one from us.

How to turn it off. On macOS, open Settings › General; on Android, open Settings › Privacy. Then switch off Share anonymous usage data. Collection is on by default, so it continues until you switch it off. Once you do, the App stops sending both its own events and Firebase's automatic ones. On Android, if the App cannot read its settings, it collects nothing. Turning it off stops any future collection; it does not retract data that was already sent before you switched it off. You can also uninstall the App at any time, which stops collection entirely.

This information is collected and processed by Google as our service provider, under its own privacy policy; see Google's Privacy Policy and Firebase Privacy and Security.

We do not use crash reporting, advertising, or attribution SDKs, and the App contains no ads.

Separately, the store you installed the App from — Apple or Google — may collect information such as downloads, purchases, and crash reports under its own privacy policy. That collection is outside our control and is not something we request or receive in identifiable form.

5. Sharing

We do not sell your personal information. The only information leaving the App is the anonymous usage data described above, which goes to Google as our service provider. We do not share it for advertising or combine it with other data. We may also disclose information where required by law.

6. Children's privacy

The App is not directed to children under the age of 13 (or the minimum age required in your jurisdiction), and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them.

7. Your rights

Depending on where you live, privacy laws such as the GDPR (European Economic Area and United Kingdom), the CCPA/CPRA (California), and similar laws elsewhere give you the right to access, correct, delete, or export personal data a company holds about you.

We hold no personal data about you, so in most cases there is nothing for us to retrieve, correct, export, or delete. Requests generally concern the anonymous analytics data held by Google as described in section 4. You can act on that directly and immediately: switch off Share anonymous usage data in the App's settings (General on macOS, Privacy on Android) to stop further collection, and reinstall the App, or on Android clear its data, to reset the app instance ID that past data was associated with. To make a request of us, or to ask us to approach Google on your behalf, contact us using the details below.

8. Changes to this policy

We may update this Privacy Policy from time to time. When we do, we will revise the "Last updated" date above. A change that collects something new will not take effect on Android without an App update that also updates the App's Data safety section on Google Play.

If you have any questions about this Privacy Policy, contact us at contact@gemiry.com.
